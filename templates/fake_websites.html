DECEPTION TACTICS

8 Common Web Deception Techniques

TACTIC 1
Typosquatting

Registering common typos, missing letters, or adjacent keyboard keys (e.g. amzon.com, paypa1.com, gooogle.com) to catch hasty visitors.

TACTIC 2
Lookalike Domains

Adding security-themed suffixes or hyphens to a legitimate brand name, such as chase-security-portal.com or apple-support-cloud.net.

TACTIC 3
Subdomain Trickery

Prepending trusted brand names as subdomains on an attacker server: login.microsoft.com.attacker-controlled.xyz. The true host is attacker-controlled.xyz!

TACTIC 4
IDN Homoglyphs

Using international Cyrillic or Greek characters that render identically to Latin letters (e.g. Cyrillic 'а' U+0430 for 'a'). To humans it looks like apple.com.

TACTIC 5
The HTTPS Padlock Fallacy

The lock icon means the connection is encrypted—it does NOT mean the website operator is honest! Over 82% of active phishing sites now possess valid SSL certificates.

TACTIC 6
Unexpected Login Prompts

Websites that immediately require entering Google or Microsoft 365 credentials before allowing you to view a simple menu, PDF, or blog post.

TACTIC 7
Counterfeit Payment Pages

E-commerce clones with broken checkout gateways that solicit payment via unrecoverable methods (wire transfers, crypto, Zelle, or sketchy direct card forms).

TACTIC 8
AitM Reverse Proxies

Toolkits like Evilginx proxy the authentic Microsoft or Google login page in real-time, capturing your 2FA session token directly as you log in.

💡 The Golden Rule of URL Safety:

Read URLs from right to left! Find the very first single slash (/) after the https://. The two words immediately to the left of that slash define the True Registered Domain. Everything before that is just a subdomain prefix that any scammer can create in seconds.
