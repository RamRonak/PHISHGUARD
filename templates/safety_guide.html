Prevention & Cyber Safety Guide

10 actionable cybersecurity best practices and an enterprise defense checklist to inoculate yourself against social engineering attacks.

Download Defense Checklist (.TXT)
RULE 1
Verify the Sender

Never rely on the friendly display name alone. Always inspect the full email address header and verify that the Reply-To address matches the legitimate corporate domain.

RULE 2
Inspect Links Before Opening

Hover over every hyperlink (or long-press on mobile) to inspect the true destination. Remember to locate the registered domain immediately preceding the first single slash (/).

RULE 3
Never Share OTPs or Passwords

No legitimate bank, IT technician, or software administrator will EVER ask you to read back a multi-factor SMS passcode or disclose your private password over the phone or via email.

RULE 4
Enable Multi-Factor Authentication

Enforce MFA on all personal and work accounts. Whenever possible, choose phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2 / Passkeys or hardware YubiKeys) over vulnerable SMS text codes.

RULE 5
Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Adopt memorable 4-word passphrases (e.g. correct-horse-battery-staple) that exceed 16 characters. Never reuse the same password across personal and enterprise accounts.

RULE 6
Keep Software Updated

Enable automatic updates on your operating system, web browsers, and endpoint security agents. Patches remediate zero-day vulnerabilities targeted by phishing payload drive-by downloads.

RULE 7
Avoid Unexpected Attachments

Treat container files (.iso, .vhd), password-protected .zip archives, and macro-enabled documents (.docm, .xlsm) with extreme suspicion. Never click "Enable Macros" on unsolicited files.

RULE 8
Out-of-Band Verification

Whenever an urgent financial wire, gift card purchase, or direct deposit change is requested, verify it via a separate communication channel (e.g. call on a known company extension).

RULE 9
Report Suspicious Messages

Use your email client's "Report Phishing" button. Early reporting allows the Security Operations Center (SOC) to purge the lure from every other employee inbox across the company.

RULE 10
Use a Password Manager

Password managers provide cryptographic domain origin verification. They will NEVER auto-fill credentials on lookalike or typosquatted domains (e.g. paypa1.com).
