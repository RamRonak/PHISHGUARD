The 7 Red Flags of a Phishing Email

1. Suspicious Sender Addresses

Display name says "IT Support", but the actual address is helpdesk99@gmail.com or a lookalike domain like @micros0ft.com. Always expand the sender header.

2. Artificial Urgency & Deadlines

Demands immediate reaction within 1 to 2 hours under threat of account termination, legal action, or payroll loss to induce panic and prevent verification.

3. High-Risk Attachments

Container files (.iso, .img), password-protected .zip files used to evade mail gateway scanners, or macro-enabled documents (.docm, .xlsm).

4. Deceptive Hyperlinks

Hyperlink text says "Click to verify", but hovering reveals a completely unrelated destination or tricky subdomain (e.g. paypal.com.scam.net).

5. Grammar & Tone Inconsistencies

Awkward phrasing, generic greetings ("Dear Customer"), or an uncharacteristically demanding tone from someone claiming to be an internal executive.

6. Solicitations for Credentials

Direct requests for your password, two-factor SMS OTP code, or prompts to "re-authenticate" your account on an unverified external form.

7. Unexpected Wire, Banking or Invoice Changes

Direct requests to wire funds, purchase gift cards, or update vendor banking direct-deposit details without secondary out-of-band telephone verification.
