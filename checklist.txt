--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. EMAIL SCREENING: THE S.L.A.M. METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] S - SENDER VERIFICATION
    * Expand the sender's full email address (not just the friendly display name).
    * Check for subtle character substitutions (e.g., 'micros0ft.com' vs 'microsoft.com').
    * Compare the 'Reply-To' header: Does it match the visible sender domain?
    * Watch for generic free webmail domains (@gmail.com, @yahoo.com) masquerading 
      as corporate or IT helpdesk entities.

[ ] L - LINK INSPECTION (NEVER BLIND CLICK)
    * Hover your cursor over any hyperlink to preview the actual destination URL.
    * Inspect the true registered domain (the part immediately before the first single '/').
    * Beware of subdomain deception (e.g., 'login.microsoft.com.attacker-controlled.net').
    * Remember: An HTTPS padlock ONLY means transit is encrypted; it does NOT mean 
      the website is legitimate or safe!

[ ] A - ATTACHMENT SCRUTINY
    * Were you expecting this attachment? If unexpected, pause immediately.
    * Never open container files (.iso, .img, .vhd) or password-protected .zip files 
      sent from external or unfamiliar senders.
    * Avoid macro-enabled documents (.docm, .xlsm) that ask you to "Enable Editing" 
      or "Enable Content".

[ ] M - MESSAGE CONTEXT & PSYCHOLOGY
    * Does the email create extreme urgency, fear of penalty, or artificial deadlines?
    * Does it request wire transfers, gift cards, password changes, or MFA codes?
    * Does it claim to be from a CEO, executive, or legal team demanding secrecy?

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. OUT-OF-BAND (OOB) VERIFICATION RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] NEVER verify a suspicious request using contact details provided INSIDE the 
    suspicious message itself.
[ ] Call the sender directly using an independently verified phone number from 
    the official company directory, intranet, or previous legitimate invoices.
[ ] For banking or routing number changes: Dual-control phone callback confirmation 
    is MANDATORY before executing any wire or payment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. AUTHENTICATION & CREDENTIAL HYGIENE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA):
    * Enable MFA on all email, banking, cloud, and work accounts.
    * Prefer phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2 / WebAuthn hardware keys or Passkeys) 
      over SMS-based codes or simple push notifications.
    * NEVER approve an MFA push notification you did not directly trigger! 
      (Beware of MFA Fatigue / Spamming attacks).
[ ] Password Management:
    * Use a reputable password manager. Password managers strictly match domain 
      origin and will NEVER auto-fill credentials on lookalike phishing domains.
    * Ensure unique 16+ character passphrases for every distinct online service.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. BROWSER & SYSTEM HYGIENE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Keep your web browser, operating system, and endpoint software updated.
[ ] Be cautious of search engine sponsored ads when searching for login portals 
    (e.g., searches for "Bank of America login" or "AnyDesk support" frequently show 
    malicious sponsored ad clones).
[ ] Manually type known official URLs into your address bar, or use verified bookmarks.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. EMERGENCY INCIDENT RESPONSE: "IF YOU CLICKED"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If you clicked a suspicious link or suspect you entered credentials on a fake site:
1. DISCONNECT IMMEDIATELY: Disconnect Ethernet cable or disable Wi-Fi to stop 
   lateral network traversal or active malware communication.
2. REPORT PROMPTLY: Notify your internal IT Helpdesk, SOC, or Security Team. 
   Organizations maintain a strict NO-BLAME policy for rapid, honest reporting!
3. RESET CREDENTIALS: From a separate, known-clean device, immediately change 
   the compromised password.
4. REVOKE SESSIONS: Access your identity provider account settings (e.g., Microsoft 365, 
   Google Workspace) and click "Sign out of all active sessions".
5. DOCUMENT: Take note of the exact time, email subject, and any information entered.
